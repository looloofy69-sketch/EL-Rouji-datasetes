# Research Index

**Last updated:** 5 November 2026  
**Current phase:** Opportunity qualification, before product commitment.

## Executive position

This research is **not yet selecting one product**. Several evolving opportunity lanes now deserve comparison: cybersecurity/data-protection operations, local cloud/compute and managed AI workloads, public-sector data governance/interoperability, sector-specific workflow systems, language technology and agent assurance.

The earlier document-operations idea has been downgraded: generic invoice extraction, duplicate detection and supplier-statement matching are already covered by local and international products. A narrower local interoperability gap may still exist, but it needs hands-on competitor testing and direct workflow evidence.

New regulation, provider activity and procurement notices point to an active digital infrastructure and security ecosystem. These are signals, not proof of an open market: laws may await implementing guidance, provider claims need testing, and tender notices are not awarded contracts.

No product commitment, representative buyer validation, paid pilot, current market-size estimate or product-market fit is established.

## Key findings

1. **AI adoption is not business transformation.** The UK 2026 representative business survey reports 32% adoption, 50% of adopters reporting a productivity increase (generally modest), and 14% rating AI highly or critically important. This is UK evidence, not an Algeria estimate. [S01]
2. **Business AI value likely requires workflow integration.** The OECD 2026 D4SME survey reports increasing off-the-shelf use but uneven strategic, targeted and secure integration. Its 2,000+ SME sample across 12 OECD countries is explicitly non-representative. [S02]
3. **Agent reliability is a deployment constraint.** An ICML 2026 study of 306 practitioners across 26 domains reports that 68% of production agents execute at most 10 steps before human intervention and 74% rely primarily on human evaluation. [S04]
4. **Algeria's online payment growth needs careful interpretation.** First-half 2026 online payments reportedly grew 52% by count and 867% by value; AADL 3, Hadj fees and Eid-related purchases were cited as major value drivers. This is not equivalent to retail e-commerce growth. [S08]
5. **Algerian language capability is promising but difficult.** CAFE provides roughly 37 hours of spontaneous Algerian Arabic/French/English code-switching speech; recent ASR results still show a substantial error gap on code-switched speech and named entities. [S10–S12]
6. **The local product field is competitive.** KIO and COD Scale market integrated commerce/COD operations; Facturili, Fisira and DZ Compta market overlapping invoicing/accounting workflows, including AI extraction claims. [S13–S17]
7. **Extraction accuracy must be evaluated on real documents.** An October 2026 EMNLP Industry Track study found synthetic documents overestimated accuracy by 38–45 percentage points on one real-document category; aggregate scores concealed large category-level differences. [S03]

## Latest competitive correction

The document-operations idea has been narrowed again. AutoEntry and Dext already offer supplier document capture, extraction, duplicate controls and supplier-statement reconciliation. The concept is therefore **not** a novel generic document-processing product. The only remaining hypothesis is a locally differentiated interoperability/exception-control workflow, pending hands-on product comparison and direct workflow evidence. [S19–S22]

This reduces confidence in the idea and strengthens the no-broad-build decision.

## Additional sector landscape update

The next pass extends the map to payments, logistics, ports/customs, construction, higher education and tourism. [Full sector assessment](17-SECTOR-LANDSCAPE-FINANCE-LOGISTICS-PUBLIC-SERVICES-TOURISM.md).

- **Payments:** PSP Regulation 25-02 and Instruction 06-2025 define the regulated perimeter. Djezzy Payment Services is authorized to constitute as a PSP, but the Bank of Algeria says separate operating approval must follow. [S89–S91]
- **Logistics:** ECOSERVICE, COD Scale and GHL target COD and fulfillment; courier-dz and DZBuild provide integration abstractions. Generic courier aggregation is not an open wedge by default. [S100–S104]
- **Ports/customs:** SERPORT describes APCS as launched in 2021; ALCES services verified here are traveler-facing declarations/vehicle TPD. APCS governance, live port coverage and freight integration remain open. [S95–S97]
- **Construction:** the public-works portal is adding contractor qualification/classification and engineering-office approval services. Check live usage and supplier contracts. [S98]
- **Higher education:** Ministry sources report 391,018 students assigned through a 100%-digital 2026 orientation process, with an LLM assistant; metrics are Ministry-reported, not independently audited. [S92–S94]
- **Tourism:** studies report payment/hotel-system fragmentation, while SAAFIR and Matares already target agency/hotel workflows. Generic agency ERP or hotel PMS is not an unoccupied category. [S99, S105–S106]

### New research implications

The public-sector digitization footprint is becoming substantial, but public platforms can be both enablers and incumbents. Search for complementary integration, migration, assurance and support needs rather than assume replacement opportunities.

Last-mile logistics and COD operations are more crowded than a simple pain-point scan suggests. Port/freight logistics is distinct and needs its own technical and governance research.

## Electronic procurement portal update — 2 November 2026

[Full portal and SME-access assessment](21-E-PROCUREMENT-PORTAL-AND-SME-MARKET-ACCESS-2026-11-02.md).

- **Legal implementation:** an interministerial order of 4 February 2026, published 2 March, defines the portal's contents and management and repeals the 2013 portal order. [S141]
- **Core functions:** registration, multi-criteria search, alerts, notice/programme/award publication, document exchange, bid upload, e-signature, timestamps, encrypted exchange, event logs, interoperability and secure archiving. [S141]
- **Supplier enablement:** DGB invites operators to register; NESDA describes electronic submission/result tracking and offers registration workshops for micro-enterprises. This is guidance, not independent end-to-end system testing. [S142–S143]
- **Commercial boundary:** the state portal is the core system. The open question is whether suppliers will pay for bid-readiness, evidence management, tender intelligence or integrations beyond the free baseline. No commercial gap has been validated.

## SME financing and investment incentives update — 2 November 2026

[Full SME financing map](20-SME-FINANCING-AND-INVESTMENT-INCENTIVES-2026-11-02.md).

- **NESDA:** advertises micro-enterprise creation/expansion financing up to DZD10m, including mixed and triangular structures with agency unremunerated-loan participation and bank lending. Eligibility, own contribution and activity rules vary by route. [S132–S134]
- **FGAR:** provides bank-credit guarantee coverage from 10% to 80% depending on project risk, with a DZD100m maximum guarantee in the described general creation/extension route. It still requires a bankable project and can coexist with ordinary bank collateral. [S135–S136]
- **CGCI-PME:** guarantees eligible tangible investment credits/leasing with a maximum eligible loan of DZD350m; published cover is 80% for creation and 60% for development, with sector exclusions. [S137–S138]
- **AAPI:** three investment regimes (priority sectors, zones and structuring projects) require advance investment registration. The structural-investment threshold includes at least 500 jobs and DZD10bn of investment. [S139–S140]

**Interpretation:** startup equity, micro-enterprise loans, SME bank guarantees and investment incentives are different financial instruments. A business model should be matched to its capital intensity, legal form, sector, eligibility and ability to service debt—not assume public support is interchangeable.

## Funding and commercialization access update — 2 November 2026

[Full funding, tax and market-access report](19-FUNDING-TAX-AND-MARKET-ACCESS-2026-11-02.md).

- **Article 119, Finance Law 2026:** Algerian-law establishments with annual turnover of at least DZD 2bn must allocate at least 1% of taxable profit to R&D/innovation, in-house or via open innovation with labelled startups/incubators. Sector scope and application modalities require regulation. The declaration service is still marked “opening soon”. [S18, S121–S122]
- **Article 147 quater, Finance Law 2025:** a separate taxable-profit allowance applies to qualifying R&D/open innovation, capped at 30% of accounting profit and DZD 200m. Article 171 was repealed; an April 2025 order updated the earlier implementing order and retained the six-month contract condition. [S117–S120]
- **Open Innovation platform:** the challenge-submission route is live, but challenge postings, matched projects, signed contracts and paid deployments have not been counted. Its homepage still contains obsolete Article 171/LF2023 language. [S120, S123–S125]
- **Startup/Scale-up labels and ASF:** the Startup label affects access to tax benefits and ASF; ASF advertises equity/quasi-equity and tickets up to DZD150m depending on project needs/maturity. Fund figures are self-reported; DZD1.3bn denotes partner introductions for pilots, not invested capital. [S126–S129]
- **Public procurement:** Law 23-12 creates two separate routes: Article 41 allows exceptional direct negotiation for labelled startups offering unique and innovative digital/innovation solutions; Article 58 requires certain needs that SMEs/TPEs/labelled startups can satisfy to be reserved for them unless justified, within a 20% maximum ceiling. Articles 110/112 clarify transition: old regulatory provisions remain where not contrary until replacement, with procedures launched before the law handled under the previous decree. Actual Article 58 tender usage and the exact 20% calculation remain unverified. [S130–S131]

**Interpretation:** enterprise open innovation could become a real buyer-acquisition route, but tax incentives, labels, funding and procurement exceptions do not guarantee a contract. Conversion to paid work must be measured.

## Procurement and national strategy update — 2 November 2026

[Full evidence update](18-PROCUREMENT-DIH-AND-CYBER-STRATEGY-SIGNALS-2026-11-02.md).

- **National cybersecurity strategy:** the accessible 2025–2029 strategy has four axes—technical/operational capacity; legal, organizational and standards framework; training/R&D/awareness; and cooperation. It explicitly identifies government, finance, public safety, energy, health, water, telecoms, transport and essential-food supply chains as sensitive sectors. [S107]
- **Digital Innovation Hubs:** proposed laureates are DIHMA (manufacturing, Algiers) and Green Innovation Hub (renewable energy, Ouargla); ASIA/Innov4Industry and ECOHUB/AFAQ are next-ranked reserves. The evaluation included service maturity, local anchoring, DMA/IMA field evidence and business-model sustainability. [S108]
- **CERIST procurement:** 2026 notices span managed cybersecurity, backup/restore, anti-DDoS, GPU equipment, SOC video-wall equipment, and renewal of Zimbra support for 5,500 existing mailboxes. The Zimbra support tender remains open until 8 November 2026, 16:00; the SOC wall deadline passed on 31 October. Award and delivery status for all listed procurement remains unverified. [S109–S115]

**Interpretation:** implementation/maintenance, integration, assurance and training may be as important as new software. DIHs may become a channel for SME diagnostics and technology delivery, but are not guaranteed buyers. Public tender notices do not establish contract awards or recurring revenue.

## Sector landscape update

The current sector pass covers agriculture, healthcare and manufacturing as three distinct markets. [Full sector map](16-SECTOR-LANDSCAPE-AGRI-HEALTH-MANUFACTURING.md).

- **Agriculture:** SNAI has launched with a harvest/threshing module; other registries and modules are planned for progressive rollout. Agripreneurs lists 35 opportunity sheets, and local providers advertise crop advice, drone monitoring and IoT irrigation. These signals show activity, not paid adoption or farm-level ROI. [S67–S77]
- **Healthcare:** the Ministry set a 15 November 2026 CHU digitization target and reported 2,355+ structures connected to fibre. The target is still in the future as of this research date; fibre connection is not proof of a live EHR. Local medical software providers already cover substantial practice/clinic/hospital features, so public rollout, integration, migration and security need investigation rather than another generic EHR. [S78–S85]
- **Manufacturing:** two studies report gradual adoption and cost/skills barriers (47 responses from 230 firms approached in one; 102 companies in another). A single SPEEDWIN case documents three separate systems across production, sales/accounting and marketing. These are directional signals, not a representative national market estimate. [S86–S88]

## Latest landscape changes — 1–2 November 2026

1. **Public-sector security has explicit organizational ownership.** Presidential Decree 26-07 requires a separate security and data-protection structure in public institutions, administrations and public bodies. It covers risk, audit, continuous monitoring, incident response, training and coordination with public procurement for security/confidentiality clauses in outsourced work. [S36]
2. **Cloud services have a defined authorization route and a visible incumbent field.** ARPCE's dated page describes application requirements and lists 20 cloud operators, with four asterisked entries whose regularization applications were being examined. Do not treat the March 2025 directory as a current licensing register. [S37]
3. **Local cloud competition is advancing.** AYRADE's stock-exchange profile reports a 1 billion DZD public offering to finance data-center expansion; Icosnet markets public cloud and GPU capacity; AventureCloudz is a Djezzy/Algeria Venture/Taubyte developer-cloud initiative; Djezzy has a later virtualization partnership with Armonika. These offers have different maturity and evidence states. [S38–S41, S45]
4. **Institutional demand signals are concrete but not award evidence.** CERIST issued 2026 consultations covering GPU computing, SOC equipment, backup/restore, anti-DDoS and managed cybersecurity. Track award and delivery outcomes separately. [S42–S44]
5. **Language resources are expanding.** NADI 2026, Arab Voices and ELNER-DZ show existing resources and benchmarks; the opportunity may be in quality, coverage, evaluation and domain adaptation rather than generic corpus creation. [S27–S30]

### Procurement implementation and observed practice — 2 November 2026

- The Government began reviewing the general implementing decree for Law 23-12 in March and finalized its examination of the draft on 30 April 2026. This is not proof of signature/publication/commencement. No final general decree was located in this research pass. [S157–S158]
- Article 107 requires electronic submission according to a timetable fixed by a Finance Minister order. The February 2026 portal order concerns Article 105, not that timetable. The Article 107 schedule was not located; vendor guidance also flags the missing timetable, but this needs a complete official-register check. [S130, S141, S159]
- Therefore CERIST's two email/fax submission examples are evidence of specified practice in two procedures only—not proof of nationwide non-adoption or legal non-compliance. [S162–S163]
- CERIST's 2025 similarity-detection consultations produced provisional awards to ABYSOFT SARL of DZD1,675,520 TTC and DZD877,030 TTC. They are not yet verified as paid, accepted or deployed contracts. [S160–S161]
- Article 41 already has a sector-specific cultural/artistic order under its first indent; this is distinct from the labelled-startup digital/innovation exception. DGB's May 2026 status statement specifically concerned startup-related implementing texts. [S131, S164]
- A supplier-service provider publicly advertises bid preparation from DZD150,000 HT, review from DZD60,000 and ongoing dossier management from DZD30,000/month. This is one vendor's price list, not a market average or verified sales. [S159]

## Procurement supplier-side market correction — 2 November 2026

[Full supplier-side and bid-to-cash assessment](22-PROCUREMENT-SUPPLIER-ECONOMY-AND-BID-TO-CASH-2026-11-02.md).

- **Tender alerts are a crowded paid category:** inspected providers advertise annual plans from about DZD7,900 to DZD35,900, with differences in sector coverage, tax treatment and services. [S144–S149]
- **Award intelligence is already commercialized:** Algeria Tenders advertises bidder/authority rankings, competitor market share, submitted prices, award values and sector/geographic analytics. [S148]
- **Manual support is already supplied:** providers advertise document retrieval, bid filing, opening-session representation and translation; NESDA offers portal onboarding/training support. [S143, S145, S147]
- **A private procurement marketplace also exists:** SAFAKAT markets tender/auction/group-buying workflows; actual liquidity and transaction volume are unverified. [S150]
- **Supplier finance is not a blank space:** CGMP and banks advertise bid/performance/advance guarantees and working-capital/receivable products. [S151–S154]
- **Revised hypothesis:** any remaining opportunity is more likely in a narrow supplier workflow—bid qualification/evidence management or post-award contract-to-cash handoffs—than another tender feed, award dashboard or marketplace. Neither is validated.
- **Portal status:** the official order specifies free downloads and a broad digital feature set, but direct technical access, live functionality, API availability and bulk-data reuse rights remain unverified. [S141–S143]
- **Article 58:** no verified reserved-lot notice or award was found in this bounded search; this is not proof of non-use.
