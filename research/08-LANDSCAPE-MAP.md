# Multi-Angle Landscape Map

**Version:** 1.0  
**As of:** 1 November 2026  
**Purpose:** prevent the research programme from narrowing prematurely around one technology or product hypothesis.

## 1. Unit of discovery

Do not research "AI opportunities" as a single category. Map a concrete intersection:

**market/geography × sector × organization type × buyer/user × workflow × pain/outcome × current alternative × enabling capability × constraints**

For example, "AI in agriculture" is too broad. A research unit could be "medium-scale greenhouse operator in northern Algeria, irrigation scheduling, farm manager as buyer, current manual/consultant method, water/energy cost as outcome, sensor/weather data availability and connectivity as constraints."

## 2. Landscape dimensions

| Dimension | Questions to map |
|---|---|
| Macro economy | Which sectors are growing, contracting, investing or being restructured? Where is purchasing power concentrated? |
| Policy and regulation | What laws, strategies, budgets, standards, public programmes and procurement priorities are changing? What is enacted versus proposed? |
| Buyers and users | Who experiences the problem, owns the budget, approves procurement, operates the system and bears failure risk? |
| Sector workflows | What repeated jobs, handoffs, records, decisions and exceptions occur from beginning to end? |
| Technology | Which capabilities are becoming feasible, cheaper, more reliable or commoditized? Which remain brittle? |
| Data | What data exists, who owns it, how is it accessed, what rights apply, how representative is it, and how fresh is it? |
| Infrastructure | Compute, cloud, hosting, connectivity, payment rails, identity, devices, power, cybersecurity and local support. |
| Competition | Direct products, adjacent platforms, international tools, in-house systems, consultants, outsourcing, spreadsheets and manual work. |
| Distribution | Procurement, resellers, accountants, integrators, banks, telecoms, associations, marketplaces and direct sales. |
| Economics | Budget owner, current cost, spend displaced, unit cost, gross margin, service burden, working capital and payment risk. |
| Organization and skills | Digital maturity, integration capacity, trust, training, change management and operator readiness. |
| Risk and trust | Safety, security, privacy, legal liability, data sovereignty, auditability and failure recovery. |
| Time and change | What is new, what is accelerating, what is delayed, and what events should trigger a fresh search? |

## 3. Sector coverage map

This is a research agenda, not a claim that every sector has a viable product opportunity.

### Public administration and public-service operators
Data interoperability, identity, workflow digitization, records, case management, document verification, citizen services, procurement and cybersecurity. Map procurement rules, authorized access, national networks and institutional ownership before considering a vendor opportunity.

### Financial services and payments
Merchant acceptance, PSPs, bank integrations, identity/KYC, fraud, disputes, settlement, reconciliation, credit and financial inclusion. Separate regulated payment activity from software that supports merchants or financial institutions.

### Commerce, retail and distribution
Demand forecasting, inventory, purchasing, order operations, courier handoffs, returns, COD, customer support, tax/accounting workflows and cash reconciliation. Test local and international products before assuming an underserved gap.

### Agriculture, fisheries and food systems
Farm operations, irrigation, input procurement, crop/harvest data, storage, cold chains, traceability, market access, price information, veterinary and fisheries workflows. Distinguish government data platforms from tools bought by farms, cooperatives, processors or distributors.

### Health and life sciences
Electronic health records, scheduling, referrals, claims, procurement, pharmacy inventory, diagnostics, patient communications and public-health reporting. Health-data sensitivity, interoperability, procurement and clinical safety are primary constraints.

### Manufacturing, energy and mining
Maintenance, asset management, quality, production planning, inventory, energy optimization, safety, industrial IoT and supplier operations. Evaluate legacy systems, OT security, downtime risk, data readiness and integration costs.

### Logistics, mobility and infrastructure
Fleet, routing, ports, customs, warehouse operations, public works, construction documentation, asset inspection and service dispatch. Observe actual field operations and paper-to-system handoffs.

### Construction, property and utilities
Permits, tenders, project controls, contractor qualification, engineering records, inspections, facilities, water and energy networks. Public portal rollout is not proof of vendor access or private demand.

### Education, tourism and professional services
Enrollment, learning administration, credentialing, language services, bookings, hospitality systems, professional compliance, records and client operations. Look for fragmented workflows and distribution channels, not simply missing apps.

## 4. Capability map

Track capabilities independently of products:
- language, speech, translation, transcription and information extraction;
- document understanding, classification, verification and provenance;
- search, retrieval, knowledge management and enterprise data access;
- workflow automation, rules, orchestration and agent systems;
- forecasting, optimization, scheduling and decision support;
- vision, inspection, anomaly detection and geospatial analysis;
- identity, signatures, seals, timestamping and trusted delivery;
- data governance, catalogs, lineage, quality and interoperability;
- cybersecurity, monitoring, audit and incident response;
- payments, reconciliation and financial data connectivity;
- edge computing, low-bandwidth operation and device integration.

For each capability, track maturity, cost, latency, accuracy, failure mode, integration burden, rights and viable deployment options.

## 5. Geographic layers

Use three connected views:
1. **Global:** technical frontier, leading products, standards, pricing and benchmark performance.
2. **Regional:** North Africa and MENA distribution, language, regulation, suppliers and cross-border expansion.
3. **Local:** Algerian institutions, buyers, procurement, language behavior, workflows, infrastructure, competition and payment realities.

A global technology breakthrough is not an Algerian market opportunity until the local buyer, deployment path and economics are established.

## 6. Opportunity types to keep open

Do not restrict discovery to SaaS. Map:
- product/software;
- infrastructure and managed services;
- integration and migration;
- data products and evaluation assets;
- compliance and assurance services;
- vertical operations/outsourcing;
- marketplaces and distribution;
- embedded finance/payment-adjacent services;
- developer tools and technical components;
- hybrid software plus human review;
- export-oriented products built from local technical strengths.

The selected business form must follow the buyer, risk, sales cycle, capital and service burden.
