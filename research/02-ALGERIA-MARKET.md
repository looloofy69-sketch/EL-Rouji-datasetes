# Algeria Digital Economy and Market Context

**Snapshot date:** 1 November 2026

## 1. Connectivity: subscriptions are not unique users

ARPCE's Q2 2026 detailed Internet observatory reports:
- 54,744,829 active mobile Internet subscriptions;
- 6,969,567 fixed Internet subscriptions;
- fixed plus mobile total of 61.71 million;
- 4.42% year-on-year growth in the overall base from Q2 2025.

The detailed mobile and fixed values sum to approximately 61.714 million, consistent with 61.71 million after rounding. ARPCE's general market-indicator landing page separately displays 61.32 million Internet subscribers for Q2 2026. This presentation discrepancy is unresolved. Counts are subscriptions, not unique persons, and can include multiple subscriptions per user. [S07]

**Implication:** digital reach is substantial. It does not establish willingness to pay for SaaS, reliable last-mile delivery, card use or uniform connectivity quality.

## 2. E-commerce: growth with structural constraints

UNCTAD's Algeria eTrade Readiness Assessment (published 20 June 2025) reports:
- registered e-commerce businesses grew at an average annual rate of 92% since 2020;
- online payment transactions for goods and services tripled between 2020 and 2024;
- B2C e-commerce sales represented 0.8% of GDP in 2023;
- around 90% of online transactions were paid cash on delivery;
- e-commerce remains mainly domestic, with formalization of last-mile delivery and stronger compliance identified as priorities.

These figures show both growth and structural constraints. The 0.8% GDP measure is a 2023 estimate, not a 2026 market-size estimate. The COD share is UNCTAD's reported estimate, not a fully audited current payment census. [S09]

A secondary report on first-half 2026 payments reports 15.43 million Internet transactions, up 52% year on year, with value near 346 billion DZD, up 867%. It attributes much of the value increase to large administrative and seasonal transactions: AADL 3 installments, Hadj fees and Eid-related purchases. This is not a clean measure of retail e-commerce expansion. Obtain the primary payment-scheme release before using these figures in a market model. [S08]

**Implication:** commerce software must account for COD, courier exceptions, returns, cash reconciliation, address quality and trust. But local products already target many of these problems; friction alone is not proof of an open gap.

## 3. SMEs and distribution

A small-business product must be affordable to very small operators and cheap to onboard. A fragmented market may have a large business count but limited purchasing power, technical readiness and support capacity.

Potential channels include accountants, sector associations, software implementers, logistics partners and existing SaaS ecosystems. This remains a hypothesis: active-practice counts, client portfolios, referral economics and access permissions have not been verified.

Do not estimate serviceable market by multiplying an SME count by an assumed subscription price. Segment by workflow volume, pain, buyer authority, digital readiness, reachable channel and support burden.

## 4. Regulation and digital accounting: precise scope matters

Finance Law 2026 is Law No. 25-17, published in Official Journal No. 88 on 31 December 2025. The official text supports these limited observations: [S18]

- **Article 111** modifies tax declaration provisions. Taxpayers under the real-profit regime or simplified regime for non-commercial professions, where the relevant tax office is equipped with IT solutions (subject to the cited statutory exception), must submit declarations electronically. Other taxpayers whose tax offices are equipped may use electronic filing. This is not universal mandatory e-filing for every taxpayer.
- **Article 109** states that provisions created by Articles 58, 74 and 81 take effect on 1 January 2027.
- Those provisions include requirements concerning software/cash-register controls for certain sales not giving rise to invoices, and disclosure to the tax administration on request of codes, data, processing and documentation by software publishers or technical operators whose products affect accounting entries. Scope depends on statutory cross-references and implementing rules.
- **Article 82** requires specified books, registers, documents, files and supporting materials maintained electronically to be preserved in electronic form; specified information, data, processing and related technical documentation are retained until expiry of the sixth year following the year to which they relate. Application and commencement for a particular taxpayer or provider should be confirmed with qualified Algerian tax counsel and DGI guidance.

Do not translate these provisions into claims of universal software certification, universal e-invoicing or blanket approval requirements without further legal analysis.

## 5. Commercial implications

- A large subscription base is not a buyer count.
- Growth in online payments is not synonymous with retail-commerce growth.
- COD pain is already a competitive product category.
- Regulation can create demand but also legal and implementation risk.
- Micro-business affordability makes high-touch onboarding and support dangerous.
- Partnerships may reduce acquisition cost but introduce channel dependency and integration obligations.
