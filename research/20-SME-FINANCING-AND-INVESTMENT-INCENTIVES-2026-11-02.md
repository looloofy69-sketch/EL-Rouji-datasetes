# SME Financing, Credit Guarantees and Investment Incentives in Algeria

**Snapshot:** 2 November 2026  
**Purpose:** map financing routes for micro-enterprises, SMEs, startups and capital-intensive investments.  
**Status:** first-pass funding map; product/business eligibility requires direct review with the relevant agency and bank.

## 1. Executive assessment

Algeria's business-financing system is not one generic "startup support" programme. It contains different instruments for different company sizes, legal forms and capital needs:

1. **Startup/innovation finance:** Startup label, ASF equity/quasi-equity, A-Venture and enterprise open innovation. Best suited to innovative, scalable products and services with a defined growth path.
2. **Micro-enterprise financing:** NESDA self-financing, mixed and triangular schemes, including unremunerated loans and an expansion programme. Designed for micro-enterprise creation/expansion, not equivalent to venture capital.
3. **Bank investment credit with guarantee support:** FGAR and CGCI-PME reduce a portion of bank credit risk, but the bank remains the lender and evaluates the borrower/project. These are guarantees, not cash grants.
4. **Investment incentives:** AAPI investment registration and its sector, zone and structuring regimes provide conditional benefits for qualifying investments, with prior registration required.
5. **Public procurement and corporate innovation contracting:** Article 41's narrow startup procurement exception and the Article 119/Article 147 quater open-innovation mechanisms create buyer-access routes, not financing entitlements.

**Commercial implication:** choose the funding route only after defining the business model. A capital-light SaaS startup, an equipment-heavy integrator, a micro-enterprise service provider and a data-center investor face different criteria, ticket sizes, diligence and repayment obligations.

## 2. NESDA: micro-enterprise creation and expansion

The National Entrepreneurship Support and Development Agency describes financing for micro-enterprises through three structures: self-financing, mixed financing and triangular financing. Its current English funding page advertises project financing up to **DZD 10 million**, unremunerated agency loans, bank co-financing and loans with 100% interest rebates. [S132]

### 2.1 Published financing structures

| Structure | Project-holder contribution | NESDA agency contribution | Bank contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Self-financing | 100% | 0% | 0% |
| Mixed financing | 50% | 50% | 0% |
| Triangular financing | 5–15%, depending on profile/region | 15–25%, depending on profile/region | 70% |

The detailed table differentiates triangular financing for students/unemployed applicants (5/25/70), non-unemployed applicants in the South (10/20/70), High Plateaus/special regions (12/18/70) and other regions (15/15/70). These are programme-published percentages; the applicant's current eligibility and project category must be confirmed by NESDA. [S132]

The funding page additionally describes:
- up to DZD 500,000 as an unremunerated loan for renting premises where the project uses bank financing at creation;
- an exceptional additional unremunerated operating loan up to DZD 1,000,000 for exploitation/operational needs. [S132]

### 2.2 NESDA activity expansion programme

The separate **NESDA Expansion** programme applies to micro-enterprises wishing to expand existing activity, launch a subsidiary activity or diversify. The current page lists:
- maximum project financing up to DZD 10 million;
- five-day entrepreneurship training;
- mixed or triangular financing;
- 100% interest rebate;
- shared eligibility conditions including age 20–58 and proof of activity during the preceding two years (accounting records/tax declarations such as G12/C20).

It accepts specified previous NESDA beneficiaries and certain non-beneficiaries (including people who started through ANGEM/CNAC, farmers, artisans and licence/registry holders), but applies additional conditions. For example, some previous beneficiaries must have repaid loans and retain active registration; non-beneficiaries must not already have received certain expansion/create support from other agencies or AAPI, and need qualifications in the activity. [S133]

### 2.3 NESDA guarantee fund

NESDA's Mutual Caution Fund (FCMG) complements bank guarantees for micro-enterprise loans issued under its mechanism. The published page says:
- a project is enrolled after bank approval;
- the micro-enterprise contribution is 0.35% annually, calculated on the bank loan and paid once;
- the bank contributes 1% of outstanding debt annually;
- after three unpaid six-month instalments and a field visit, the bank may be compensated for up to 70% of the remaining amount; the fund then follows up with the distressed micro-enterprise. [S134]

This is a repayment-risk mechanism attached to NESDA-supported lending, not a general guarantee for all SMEs or commercial loans.

### 2.4 When NESDA may fit

Potentially suitable for:
- small-scale software/services business with an eligible operational project;
- local installation, maintenance, digitization or support activity;
- equipment purchase and setup for a small productive/service enterprise;
- expansion of an existing eligible micro-enterprise.

Do not assume a software startup automatically qualifies. Confirm the current activity list, required founder contribution, asset restrictions, training, repayment terms and bank participation before using this route in an investment plan.

## 3. FGAR: guarantee support for bank credit

The **Fonds de Garantie des Crédits aux PME (FGAR)** provides a financial guarantee to the bank for eligible SME investment loans. It is intended to address insufficient collateral/guarantees and facilitate access to medium-term bank credit or leasing. The bank remains the lender. [S135]

### 3.1 General guarantee characteristics

FGAR's published terms state:
- guarantee coverage between **10% and 80%** of credit, determined per project according to cost and risk;
- maximum guarantee amount of **DZD 100 million** for projects in creation or extension under the relevant route;
- maximum term of seven years for classic investment credit and ten years for leasing;
- a detailed FGAR-format business plan is required;
- bank collateral/security over financed assets may still be required;
- final guarantee is issued only after the bank has notified the promoter of its financing approval;
- FGAR charges a study commission and an engagement premium. [S135]

A separate FGAR/MEDA route has different eligibility and limits. The published criteria describe PME in development, private-sector companies installed in Algeria, with at least one year of activity for the MEDA route; it excludes financing for new company creation or business transmission. Its stated coverage ceiling and eligible uses should not be conflated with the general FGAR creation/extension route. [S136]

### 3.2 What it means commercially

FGAR is relevant when the business needs bank-financed equipment, industrial assets, expansion or eligible working-capital components linked to investment. It does not fund product development as equity and does not guarantee that a bank will approve a loan. The project must be bankable and the guarantee must be accepted by FGAR.

## 4. CGCI-PME: a separate bank-credit guarantee mechanism

The **Caisse de Garantie des Crédits d'Investissement (CGCI-PME)** guarantees eligible bank investment credits for SMEs. It does not lend directly to the company; the guarantee is granted to the credit institution and does not replace ordinary bank collateral. [S137]

The CGCI-PME page/FAQ lists:
- eligible tangible investment credit with initial repayment term up to seven years;
- movable equipment leasing up to ten years;
- maximum eligible credit of **DZD 350 million**;
- coverage of **80%** for a company in creation (described as operating for no more than one year) and **60%** for expansion/development/equipment renewal;
- coverage base capped at DZD 250 million, which implies maximum covered amounts of DZD 200 million for creation and DZD 150 million for development;
- maximum cumulative CGCI engagement per beneficiary of DZD 250 million. [S137]

### 4.1 Sector exclusions and important constraints

CGCI's published SME guarantee criteria exclude:
- commercial activities;
- agriculture;
- fishing;
- companies with un-repaid loans under the former ANSEJ, CNAC or ANGEM support schemes;
- projects already benefiting from another financial guarantee for the same credit.

Its SME eligibility definition uses turnover, balance-sheet and employee thresholds; the public page describes turnover not exceeding DZD 4 billion or balance sheet not exceeding DZD 1 billion and a workforce not exceeding 250, subject to the full legal criteria. [S137]

CGCI does not replace bank collateral or constitute a prior condition for the bank to grant credit. It is a risk-sharing support mechanism, not a loan subsidy.

### 4.2 Separate CGCI TPE/PE delegated guarantee

CGCI also describes a delegated guarantee for very small/small enterprises, with decision delegated to bank regional offices/agencies to simplify access; the advertised delegated credit limit is DZD 50 million. Its detailed enterprise thresholds and eligible categories must be read separately from the CGCI-PME product. [S138]

## 5. AAPI: investment-registration incentives

The Algerian Investment Promotion Agency (AAPI) lists three investment incentive regimes under Law No. 22-18:
1. **Priority sectors:** mining/quarrying; agriculture, aquaculture and fisheries; industry/agro-food/pharmaceutical/petrochemical activities; services and tourism; renewable energy; and knowledge economy/ICT.
2. **Zones:** areas including the Highlands, South and Greater South, localities needing particular state support and localities with natural resources to be developed.
3. **Structuring investments:** projects with significant wealth/job-creation and spillover potential, subject to further criteria. [S139]

### 5.1 Registration is a prerequisite

AAPI says investments must be registered with the competent one-stop shop or via the investor platform **before realization** to obtain benefits. Registration produces a certificate and a list of eligible goods/services that can be presented to relevant administrations. [S139]

The DGI's published AAPI criteria include a structuring-investment threshold of at least **500 direct jobs** and an investment amount of at least **DZD 10 billion**. These criteria make the structuring regime irrelevant to most small software startups, although priority-sector or zone regimes may be relevant to other eligible investments. [S140]

### 5.2 What this mechanism is and is not

AAPI incentives relate to an eligible registered **investment project**, not automatically to an ordinary software company's ongoing operating expenses. Do not assume that every ICT/software company is eligible for every incentive. Confirm the applicable regime, eligible activities, excluded activities, approved equipment/services, timing and obligations before purchase or project implementation.

## 6. Funding and financing routes compared

| Instrument | Main beneficiary/use | Form of support | Main gate / constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASF | Labelled innovative startup | Equity/quasi-equity and post-investment support | Startup label; investment committee assessment; discretionary |
| Article 147 quater open innovation | Large enterprise contracting labelled startup/incubator | Taxable-profit allowance for eligible R&D/open innovation | Qualifying expenses; compliant contract; six-month minimum; evidence and tax review |
| Article 119 R&D/innovation allocation | Algerian-law establishments with turnover >= DZD2bn | Mandatory annual allocation; possible demand for startup partnerships | Sector/application regulations, taxable profit base, reporting service and label scope |
| NESDA | Eligible micro-enterprise creation/expansion | Unremunerated agency loan and bank co-financing | Founder age/contribution, eligible activity, feasibility, bank and agency approval |
| FCMG | NESDA-financed micro-enterprise debt | Shared bank-default risk | Applies within NESDA mechanism; contribution and default rules |
| FGAR | SMEs seeking bank investment credit | Guarantee to bank, generally 10–80% | Bank approval, project analysis, plan and guarantee fees; does not remove collateral automatically |
| CGCI-PME | SMEs seeking eligible tangible investment credit/leasing | Guarantee to bank, typically 60–80% within caps | Sector exclusions and credit/borrower criteria |
| AAPI | Qualifying investment projects | Conditional tax/customs/other investment advantages | Prior registration; regime, sector/zone/scale and approved asset eligibility |
| Article 41 public procurement | Labelled startup with unique/innovative digital/innovation solution | Possible direct-negotiated public contract | Exceptional route, implementing rules and contracting authority discretion |

## 7. What this changes in the opportunity map

### Capital-light software and services

Potential routes:
- Startup label and ASF for an innovative/scalable product;
- open-innovation contracts with large enterprises;
- paid ordinary B2B pilots;
- Article 41 public procurement only where its narrow legal requirements are satisfied.

This route is less capital-intensive but depends on validated buyer demand, a label, credible delivery capacity, security/privacy compliance and working capital to survive enterprise payment cycles.

### Equipment-heavy integration and managed services

Potential routes:
- bank investment credit supported by FGAR or CGCI-PME;
- AAPI investment registration where the project and assets meet an eligible regime;
- ordinary vendor financing, leasing or supplier terms.

This route is more capital-intensive and requires a bankable business plan, equipment/vendor quotations, collateral/security, maintenance capabilities and predictable utilization or contracted demand.

### Micro-enterprise delivery

Potential routes:
- NESDA creation/expansion financing for qualifying micro-enterprise activities;
- small-scale equipment/service deployment;
- partnerships/subcontracting with integrators, DIHs or larger vendors.

This route has different founder eligibility and project-size boundaries from the Startup-label/ASF route.

## 8. Important distinctions and failure modes

1. **Guarantee is not credit.** FGAR/CGCI guarantee the bank's risk; the company remains responsible for the loan.
2. **Unremunerated agency loan is not a grant.** NESDA's loan and bank portions still have eligibility, repayment and default conditions.
3. **Investment incentive is not a startup tax exemption.** AAPI incentives apply to approved investment projects and specific eligible assets/services.
4. **Startup label is not funding approval.** ASF, AAPI, NESDA and bank mechanisms each have distinct gates.
5. **No financing route substitutes for customer economics.** Subsidized credit can still produce an unviable company if demand, cash collection and service margins are poor.
6. **Working capital matters.** Bank-backed equipment and enterprise contracts can create cash-flow stress due to upfront costs, delayed payment and support obligations.
7. **Instrument pages may be revised.** Eligibility, contribution percentages, guarantee limits and legal conditions should be confirmed with the relevant institution before application.

## 9. Priority follow-up

1. Map NESDA eligibility for a software/services project versus equipment-based digitalization and installation businesses.
2. Confirm bank-by-bank access, eligible activities and documentation for FGAR and CGCI; compare costs, security and approval times.
3. Clarify AAPI eligibility for ICT/software, local cloud, GPU infrastructure, cybersecurity and data-center investments, including eligible asset lists and pre-registration timing.
4. Identify banks with SME, leasing, startup or technology investment products and compare actual quotes and security requirements.
5. Trace real approved/financed projects and beneficiaries for NESDA, FGAR, CGCI and AAPI—not just programme descriptions.
6. Test whether the planned business model should be capital-light SaaS, integration/managed services or asset-backed infrastructure before selecting the financing route.

## 10. Current decision

**Treat funding access as a separate diligence layer for every opportunity.** Startup equity, open innovation, micro-enterprise loans, bank-credit guarantees and AAPI investment incentives serve different company profiles and cost structures. Do not model any public support as guaranteed capital or assume that eligibility for one instrument transfers to another.
