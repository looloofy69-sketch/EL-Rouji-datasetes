# Algeria Cybersecurity Services and Assurance Landscape

**Research snapshot:** 1 November 2026  
**Status:** market map in progress; no market-size estimate or market-entry decision.

## 1. Executive assessment

The Algerian cybersecurity services market is not empty and should not be treated as one undifferentiated SOC/pentest opportunity. Public evidence shows at least five overlapping layers:

1. **Telco, cloud and IT integrators** bundle connectivity, hosting, security products, managed services and enterprise transformation.
2. **Specialist cybersecurity/GRC providers** sell audits, penetration testing, incident response, SOC/MDR, governance, compliance and training.
3. **Payment-system evaluation bodies** are listed by GIE Monétique for specific, scoped work in the interbank payment ecosystem.
4. **International assurance and certification firms** cover ISO, PCI DSS, privacy, continuity and assessment/certification.
5. **Education and workforce institutions** develop cybersecurity engineers, analysts, auditors and incident-response specialists.

The market signal is stronger than “new regulation creates demand”: Decree 26-07 defines security/data-protection structures inside public bodies and links them to public procurement; CERIST published a 2026 outsourced cybersecurity service consultation. But implementation, awards, recurring budgets and supplier eligibility remain unverified.

**Current conclusion:** do not enter generic pentesting, basic managed firewall, commodity SOC or generic ISO preparation without a differentiated buyer/workflow/channel. Investigate narrower service packages and productized operations only after mapping incumbents, accreditation scopes, procurement access and willingness to pay.

### B. Specialist cybersecurity and GRC providers

#### Intervalle Technologies

Intervalle markets three complementary business units: Cybersecurity & GRC, Business Solutions/automation, and Banking Solutions. Its cybersecurity portfolio covers audit, compliance, integration, managed SOC and training. It advertises:
- penetration testing, code review, cloud, Active Directory, data-center and PKI audits;
- ISO 27001 information-security management systems, risk mapping, business continuity and disaster recovery;
- SOC/MDR, SIEM/EDR/XDR, incident response, vulnerability management, threat intelligence and executive reporting;
- 24/7 managed SOC, with advertised MTTD below 15 minutes and MTTR below one hour.

The company also advertises 300+ organizations and 15+ countries served. These figures, SLA metrics and certifications are company claims, not independently audited results. The official GIE Monétique register lists Intervalle among its level-3 evaluation bodies for specified payment-system audits. [S50–S52]

**Strategic significance:** Intervalle is a strong model of the integrated-services company: consulting/audit leads to implementation, business automation, banking software and managed operations. Its combination of regulated-domain knowledge and proprietary software is more defensible than a stand-alone generic pentest offering.

#### UNIDEES

UNIDEES describes itself as an Algerian cybersecurity-focused company, offering consulting, technical expertise, SOC/operations, security products and training. Its public pages discuss application/code review, architecture, simulated attacks, infrastructure penetration testing and SOC services. It states that it has achieved ISO 27001 certification for its CSIRT and Managed SOC scope; the certificate scope, issuing body, accreditation and validity should be inspected before relying on the claim. [S54]

The GIE Monétique register lists UNIDEES as a level-3 evaluator for information-system security audits. The same register shows an expiry date of 9 February 2026 while still displaying “Valid”; this is a register-status discrepancy that must be confirmed with GIE. [S50]

#### CYBEARS

CYBEARS presents itself as an Algerian cybersecurity ecosystem with services, knowledge, research, products and community. Its product/service families include:
- **Cyblock:** penetration testing, SOC, cyber-threat intelligence and threat hunting;
- **Cybuild:** security architecture and infrastructure;
- **Cybrain:** governance, risk and compliance, including ISO 27001 and personal-data frameworks;
- **CX3:** a cybersecurity command-center product/visibility layer.

The company website provides a Hydra, Algiers contact address. It advertises sector coverage and 24/7 monitoring, but detailed operating scope, customer outcomes, product deployment and staffing require direct verification. GIE Monétique lists CYBEARS in a pilot phase for evaluation/training scopes, not as an unrestricted or fully approved general cybersecurity auditor. [S50, S53]

#### Keystone Algérie

The GIE Monétique register lists SARL KEYSTONE ALGERIE at level 3 for specified information-security management-system, PCI DSS, ISO/IEC 27001 and mobile-payment security/evaluation scopes, including functional/technical assessment of the mobile switch and security audit. Its public directory profile additionally advertises penetration testing, SI audits, application/architecture audit, threat intelligence, hardening, managed CERT and digital forensics. The directory profile is not proof that every service is covered by GIE accreditation. [S50, S66]

#### MN Advising Algérie

The GIE Monétique register lists MN Advising at level 3 across organizational, physical/environmental security, access, asset protection, system acquisition/development/maintenance, incident/continuity and compliance audits, as well as several mobile-payment and switch evaluation/security scopes. These are the register's specific accreditation scopes; they should not be generalized beyond them. [S50]

#### 3S Pay

The GIE register lists 3S Pay for payment-system evaluation, including payment-terminal protocol integration and mobile-payment assessment. The page also records a pilot-phase audit/3DS/PCI DSS scope in partnership with an Indian firm. This is specialized payment testing/evaluation evidence, not proof of a broad managed-security business. The register contains expiry/status inconsistencies for an older pilot entry. [S50]

#### CyberWave

CyberWave markets penetration testing, Red/Purple Team, SOC, incident response, GRC and training, including an Algeria service page. Its LinkedIn company profile describes it as founded in 2024, Paris-headquartered and one employee; its own site claims 150+ missions, 50+ organizations and operations across eight countries. Those self-reported figures and the LinkedIn profile may be incomplete or stale. No Algerian-incorporated entity or local accreditation was confirmed in this pass. Treat it as a cross-border boutique/competitor lead, not a confirmed domestic incumbent. [S58]

### C. International certification and assurance

**SGS Algeria** advertises information-security and cybersecurity services including ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27701, ISO 22301, cloud security, PCI DSS and SOC services. Its Algeria page lists an office in Hydra, Algiers. The relevant distinction is whether the engagement is certification, audit, advisory, testing or managed operations; these are different scopes and must not be conflated. [S64]

**Forvis Mazars Algeria** appears in the GIE Monétique register at level 3 for specified physical/environmental security, access, IT-asset and mobile-payment security-conformity evaluation scopes. This provides evidence of participation in a regulated payment evaluation ecosystem, not proof of every global Mazars cybersecurity service being delivered locally. [S50]

### D. Education and workforce supply

The École Nationale Supérieure de Cybersécurité publishes a curriculum/role map spanning network and system security, SOC analysis, RSSI functions, cyber-defence, auditing, consulting, financial-sector security and penetration testing. This confirms a formal specialist talent pipeline is being built; it does not establish current graduate counts, placement rates or whether firms can recruit the experienced staff needed for 24/7 coverage. [S61]

UNIDEES also markets RSSI, ISO 27001, risk-management and security training. CYBEARS advertises awareness and technical training. Training is therefore both a provider service and a market-capability signal. [S53–S54]

## 3. Formal accreditation: understand the scope

GIE Monétique publishes a register of approved evaluation bodies. The published list includes Intervalle Technologies, MN Advising, Keystone, CYBEARS, 3S Pay, UNIDEES and Mazars, with accreditation numbers, levels, scopes, expiry dates and statuses. The GIE's process describes an application dossier containing qualifications/certifications, methods and tools, followed by review, possible pilot work with a GIE member or stakeholder, assessment of the pilot and a decision on the accreditation scope. [S50–S51]

Important limitations:
- This is accreditation for specified evaluation/testing and payment-system homologation contexts under GIE Monétique.
- It is **not** a universal national licence to provide every cybersecurity service.
- “Pilot phase” is not equivalent to full level-3 accreditation.
- A register row's “Valid” label should be cross-checked against its stated expiry date. The page currently shows at least two entries with past expiry dates while retaining “Valid” (3S Pay's pilot record and UNIDEES). Its publication/update date is not clearly stated in the page inspected.
- Verify scope and current validity directly with the GIE before relying on any credential in a proposal or procurement qualification.

## 4. What the public-sector procurement evidence actually says

Presidential Decree 26-07 defines an independent security and personal-data protection structure in public bodies and explicitly links it to procurement coordination for information-security and confidentiality clauses in outsourced work. This is a statutory organization/process signal, not an automatic purchase order. [S36]

CERIST's public procurement page lists a 2026 consultation for 12-month managed cybersecurity (audit, continuous monitoring and incident response), a SOC video-wall purchase, anti-DDoS service, backup/restore and GPU procurement. [S62]

**Critical document-integrity warning:** the 2026 consultation page for cybersecurity managed services links to a PDF whose extracted text appears to end with a submission deadline of 30 October 2024. The 2026 page and linked attachment are inconsistent. The attachment also contains detailed technical requirements (OpenStack scope, firewall/WAF, continuous monitoring, SIEM dashboard, incident-response coverage and personnel credentials), but these must **not** be treated as confirmed 2026 requirements until CERIST supplies the correct current document. The discrepancy is recorded as unresolved; the 2026 webpage establishes the notice, not the validity of the old attachment. [S62–S63]

The public information reviewed does not establish the winning bidder, contract value, acceptance, delivery, service quality or renewal. These must be searched separately.

## 5. Pricing and business-model evidence

A secondary Algerian article published in 2026 reports indicative prices for security audits and Red Team services (including 150,000 DZD, 250,000 DZD and 500,000 DZD examples). These are unverified media-reported figures, not a vendor quote, representative price survey or reliable market benchmark. Use them only as interview/quotation leads. [S65]

Potential delivery models include fixed-scope audit/pentest, GRC implementation, recurring managed monitoring/MDR/SOC, incident-response retainer, cloud/security managed services, certification-readiness advisory, training, product resale/integration, payment-system evaluation under a specific GIE scope, and combined software plus managed service.

Unit economics differ sharply. A 24/7 SOC requires staffing rotations, tooling, detection engineering, escalation, incident responsibility and evidence retention. A one-off audit requires qualified people and references but not the same standing operational capacity. Productized software does not remove trust, qualification or liability requirements.

## 6. Competitive implications and possible gaps

### Categories already contested

- basic firewall, antivirus, vulnerability scans and anti-DDoS;
- infrastructure/hosting monitoring and backup;
- conventional pentests and infrastructure/app audits;
- ISO 27001 readiness and GRC advisory;
- SOC/MDR and incident response;
- payment-system evaluation through scoped GIE-accredited providers.

### Potentially differentiated areas to test

1. **Regulation-to-operations tooling:** map Decree 26-07 obligations to risk registers, controls, audits, subcontractor clauses, incident reporting and evidence; validate whether public organizations need an external system or are implementing internally.
2. **Affordable managed security for a defined SME/sector:** a narrowly bounded, repeatable service with transparent coverage, escalation and pricing; test whether telecoms and existing integrators already meet the need.
3. **Industrial/OT security:** specialized assessment for utilities, factories, oil/gas, health facilities and critical industrial systems; verify demand, competence requirements and procurement eligibility.
4. **Cloud-native security and AI workload assurance:** hardening, identity, data controls, model endpoints, logging, prompt-injection testing and incident response for local cloud/AI deployments. Buyer budget is unknown.
5. **Security evidence/assurance automation:** evidence collection, control mapping, audit trails and continuous posture reporting; differentiate from consultants' existing software and global GRC platforms.
6. **Payment integration testing:** only if the team can qualify under the GIE's specific approval process; existing approved bodies create direct competition and a barrier.
7. **Talent and operational enablement:** SOC training, practical cyber ranges and RSSI enablement; demand, employer sponsorship and certification recognition need validation.

No gap is confirmed by the public feature scan. Each needs buyer interviews, procurement data, competitor demonstrations and cost-to-serve validation.

## 7. Priority diligence checklist

1. Obtain direct confirmation from GIE Monétique of current accreditation status, scope and expiry for each listed evaluation body.
2. Inspect the current national cybersecurity strategy and ASSI public guidance; clarify which obligations, controls, supplier qualifications and reporting routes apply.
3. Confirm whether the 2026 CERIST cybersecurity attachment is wrong/obsolete; obtain the correct cahier des charges, bidder list, award, acceptance and renewal if available.
4. Request comparable written quotes for a fixed-scope SI audit, web/mobile pentest, cloud audit, SOC/MDR monthly service, incident-response retainer and industrial/OT assessment.
5. Compare real service scope: telemetry sources, on-call coverage, local hosting, analyst qualifications, response times, SLA credits, incident liability, log retention, data export and customer references.
6. Identify the customer's actual security owner, budget holder, procurement route and internal staff capacity.
7. Separate general cyber services from GIE payment-system evaluation and from accredited ISO certification.
8. Measure the total operating cost of 24/7 service before proposing a managed SOC product.

## 8. Update — national strategy and procurement evidence, 2 November 2026

The full National Information Systems Security Strategy 2025–2029 is now available. It defines four axes: technical-operational capability; legal/organizational/normative framework; training/R&D/awareness; and national/international cooperation. Its named sensitive sectors include government, finance, public safety, energy, health, water, telecommunications, transport and essential-food production/distribution. This broadens the target-sector map but is strategic policy, not proof of budgets or tenders. [S107]

CERIST's 2026 procurement trail is now more detailed:
- managed cybersecurity for 12 months (02/CERIST/2026);
- backup/restore with three-year publisher support and training;
- managed anti-DDoS;
- GPU compute hardware;
- SOC video-wall equipment;
- one-year support renewal for 5,500 Zimbra mailboxes (deadline 8 November 2026). [S109–S115]

These line items show distinct procurement categories and maintenance requirements. The Zimbra tender is particularly useful evidence of support/upgrade work on an existing enterprise platform. Zimbra 10.1.21 was released on 24 September 2026 with additional security fixes, but the tender's reference to 10.1.16 does not establish the installation's current patch state. [S110, S114]

The 2026 cybersecurity consultation's linked PDF still appears to carry a 2024 deadline; the primary specification must be corrected/confirmed before treating its technical details as valid 2026 requirements. [S62–S63]

## 9. Current decision

**Continue cybersecurity market research; do not yet enter as a generic cyber consultancy or SOC provider.**

The evidence shows formal public-sector security roles, active procurement signals, a visible local provider ecosystem and narrow accreditation pathways. It does not yet show recurring budget, how often public structures outsource, contract values, incumbent service quality, or whether a new entrant can qualify and operate profitably.

The next step is to reconstruct the procurement and accreditation market (awards, scopes, prices, provider references), then compare one or two narrow service opportunities against incumbent delivery—not to build a dashboard or SOC product first.
