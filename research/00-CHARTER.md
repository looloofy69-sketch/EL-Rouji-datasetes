# Research Charter and Method

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 1 November 2026  
**Workstream:** EL-Rouji research intelligence

## Objective

Identify opportunities where a real recurring problem, a budgeted buyer, deployable technology, defensible differentiation and sustainable delivery economics converge. The goal is to reduce uncertainty and make falsifiable decisions, not to generate a large list of AI ideas.

## Scope

- AI adoption and operational integration;
- software engineering and AI-assisted development;
- models, inference and deployment economics;
- agents, orchestration, evaluation, observability and security;
- Algeria and North Africa digital markets;
- Arabic, Algerian Darija, French, English and code-switching technology;
- regulatory and data-governance context;
- competition, economics, distribution and product opportunities.

International evidence may inform patterns but must not be presented as proof of Algerian demand without local validation.

## Method

1. Define the decision the research must inform.
2. Prefer primary sources: legislation, official statistics, papers, standards, original datasets and product documentation.
3. Use credible secondary sources for context and triangulation.
4. Separate vendor claims from independent evidence.
5. Check dates, geography, sample size, denominator and representativeness.
6. Search for counter-evidence and substitutes.
7. Separate observation, inference, assumption and recommendation.
8. State confidence and what would change the conclusion.
9. Convert important unknowns into experiments or explicit no-go conditions.

## Evidence quality and confidence

A source can be authoritative and still not answer the exact question. Telecom subscriptions are not unique people; foreign AI surveys do not measure Algerian adoption; feature pages do not prove product quality or outcomes.

Confidence:
- **High:** multiple independent, directly relevant and methodologically credible sources agree.
- **Medium:** credible evidence supports the claim, but scope or applicability is limited.
- **Low:** sparse, indirect, self-reported, vendor-supplied or materially uncertain evidence.

## Decision discipline

For every material opportunity, record:
- target user and buyer;
- exact job-to-be-done and current workflow;
- frequency, severity and cost of the problem;
- current alternatives and switching cost;
- technical feasibility and failure modes;
- integration and support burden;
- distribution path;
- pricing hypothesis and unit economics;
- downside and opportunity cost;
- kill criteria and evidence needed.

Interest, praise, waitlist signups, demos and free pilots are not willingness-to-pay evidence. Prefer observed workflows, paid pilots, measurable savings and renewal.
